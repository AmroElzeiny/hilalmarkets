Trust is a product system—not a badge.

Trust framework

Six areas protect the user experience.

Sharia governance

Approved methodologies, qualified reviewers, versioned decisions, and visible disagreement.

Evidence integrity

Dated sources, hashes or stable references, status history, and immutable historical receipts.

Application security

Authenticated routes, RBAC, secret isolation, CSRF protection, rate limits, and audit records.

Market-data integrity

Provider identity, candle time, freshness, completeness, deterministic calculations, and incident records.

Explainable monitoring

Exact Watch Plan version, required and actual values, missing conditions, and delivery state.

User control

No automatic execution, no trading-key requirement, explicit approval, and reversible preferences.

Privacy by design

Do not send private strategy text or personal data to analytics.

First-party product events should use internal identifiers, safe event names, and minimal properties. Authentication data, credentials, wallet keys, raw prompts, detailed Watch Plan text, evidence-review notes, and support attachments must stay out of public analytics.

Safety boundaries

What the platform must never imply.

BoundaryRequired product behavior
No AI religious rulingsAI may organize evidence; approved human governance owns final methodology and status decisions.
No executionThe platform monitors and explains. It does not place orders or request trading permissions.
No profit promiseDo not market readiness, monitoring, or alerts as a guaranteed return or win probability.
Fail closedMissing or stale required screening data excludes the asset instead of silently treating it as eligible.
Preserve historyHistorical alerts retain the exact methodology, assessment, Watch Plan, and evidence context.
Separate disagreementDo not collapse approved methodology differences into false consensus.
Reporting

Security issues need a dedicated route.

Do not publish sensitive operational details on the general contact page. Production should define a protected reporting address or form, scope, acknowledgment process, and disclosure policy.
