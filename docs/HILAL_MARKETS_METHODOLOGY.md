# Hilal Markets Methodology — the automated screen

Built 30 August 2026. **Published 31 August 2026.** Automated, still under development,
and **no Shariah advisor stands behind it.**

This is a fourth methodology, standing beside the three imported authorities and never
merged into them. It exists to propose eligible coins at a scale no Shariah board
publishes at. Every result it produces must carry the notice in
`templates/hilal/partials/automated_methodology_notice.html`.

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| The rule | `services/sharia_automated_screen.py` |
| The conditions and their evidence | `services/sharia_conditions.py` |
| Reading a project's pages | `services/sharia_evidence_screen.py` |
| **What is published, and who is admitted** | `services/hilal_methodology.py` |
| The coin list a person edits | `services/hilal_methodology_admissions.json` |
| The public page | `/hilal-methodology` |

System code: `HILAL_MARKETS_METHODOLOGY` · version `2026.08-hm.1`

## Published, and kept out of two places on purpose

`scripts/publish_hilal_methodology.py --write` creates the methodology row and one
assessment per coin. It decides nothing: the admissions file is what decides, and that
file is in git, generated by `scripts/build_hilal_admissions.py` from readings that
actually happened.

The standard is **selectable but never automatic**. A person may pick it and read what a
machine made of a project's own website. It is excluded from exactly two places, and both
exclusions protect a reader who did *not* pick it:

| Where | Why |
|---|---|
| `ALL_APPROVED_METHODOLOGIES` | The aggregate picks one winner per coin across every standard. Left in, a machine reading would win outright for every coin no authority has ever assessed — most of the market — and the aggregate is the one view where a reader cannot tell whose answer they are reading. |
| `default_methodology()` | It orders by the newest effective date, and this standard is by construction the newest row. Publishing it would have made it everybody's default the same day. `SHARIA_DEFAULT_METHODOLOGY_CODE` happens to be set today; a rule that holds only while a setting is filled in is not a rule. |

Both are `hilal_methodology.is_automated`, imported rather than restated, and both are
held by `tests/unit/test_invariant_hilal_methodology.py` and
`tests/services/test_hilal_methodology_publication.py`.

**No admitted coin is plain `eligible`.** They are
`ELIGIBLE_WITH_QUALIFICATIONS`, and one of the qualifications is the warning itself. A
reader scanning a list sees the status word long before any notice, and a machine reading
must not wear the word an authority's decision wears.

25 coins as at 31 August 2026: 21 admitted, 1 refused, 3 with too little to read.

| Route | Coins | How they got in |

| Automated screen | 6 | ZEC, USD1, PUMP, RLUSD, ZKC, PROM — read from their own pages, refused by nothing. |
| Refused | 1 | ENSO. Its own documentation describes borrowing against collateral on lending protocols, across 22 of its own pages. |

The ten new coins were chosen by rule, not by taste: the highest 24-hour volume on

a coin-on-an-exchange, and `test_an_admission_record_never_names_an_exchange` keeps it
that way.

## The rule

## The rule

Facts in, verdict out. What refuses an asset is **not written in the screen** — it is
derived from the conditions the product owner has approved in
[the screening register](SHARIA_SCREENING_CONDITIONS_AR.md)
(`services/sharia_conditions.py`), each with the verse or hadith behind it.

**68 of the 80 conditions are approved.** They group into 23 kinds of business that
refuse a coin, up from the 5 the screen applied before 30 August 2026. The full list,
with its evidence, is in the Arabic register; the shape of it is:

| Chapter | Approved | What it refuses |
|---|---:|---|
| الربا — riba | 15 | Lending at interest, guaranteed returns, conventional banking and insurance, bonds, rate swaps |
| الميسر — maysir | 9 | Casinos, betting, lotteries, prediction markets, paid loot boxes, paid random draws |
| الغرر — gharar | 11 | Perpetuals and options, short selling, selling before possession, debt trading, unbacked metal claims |
| المحرمات — prohibited trades | 12 | Alcohol, pork, tobacco, narcotics, adult content, sorcery, idols, unlawful arms |
| صيغة العقد — contract form | 5 | Two sales in one, a loan tied to a sale, guaranteed capital in a partnership |
| المعايير الكمية — ratios | 4 | Forbidden income over 5%, interest-bearing debt or investments over 30% |
| الضرر — harm | 4 | Front-running users, using customers' assets, selling their data |
| الصرف — exchange | 3 | Currency for currency without immediate exchange, forwards |
| أكل المال بالباطل | 4 | Hoarding, vote buying, marketing a way to hide money |
| الغش — deceit | 1 | Coordinated pumps |

The remaining **12 change nothing**. They are read on every coin so their effect can be
seen before they are approved, and only an entry in `sharia_condition_decisions.json` —
signed, dated, and visible in git — makes one of them bite. Writing a rule down and
applying it are two separate acts, on purpose.

### What the screen reaches, and what it does not attempt

**Its reach is eighty of a project's own pages.** That number is
`coin_evidence_crawler.DEFAULT_PAGE_BUDGET`, and it is the definition of the boundary,
not a performance setting. A rule that cannot be settled from eighty pages of the
project describing itself is a rule this screen does not attempt.

**Twelve approved rules fall outside it.** Seven need a person — the form of a contract,
an audit, the mechanism behind a swap — and five need financial figures no coin data
provider publishes. They are **skipped**, silently, per coin.

They were briefly reported on every verdict as *unchecked*. That was reversed on
31 August 2026 for a plain reason: nobody could act on it. Neither the product owner nor
a Shariah provider can settle riba al-fadl from a queue entry, so the queue was work that
could never be cleared — and a flag identical on every coin carries no information. The
reach is stated **once** instead: in this note, and in the notice beside every result.

**Skipped is never passed.** A rule out of reach contributes nothing in either
direction. It has no phrases, so no page can trigger it, and
`test_a_rule_out_of_reach_is_skipped_and_never_counted_as_passed` holds both halves:
it is not raised as a question, and it never appears as a reason for a verdict.

### A conclusion drawn from silence needs enough silence to be one

Two of the screen's answers are conclusions from **absence**, and both were reachable
from a single page until 31 August 2026:

- *eligible* — nothing refused it;
- *the holder is paid nothing* — no page said otherwise.

Neither is a statement about the project until enough of the project's own writing has
been read. The token `U` was admitted as eligible on the strength of **one** marketing
homepage: the screen decided a stablecoin pays its holders nothing because a page selling
the peg did not discuss yield. That is exactly the failure `HolderReturn` exists to
prevent — Dai, GHO, USD0, AUSD and FRXUSD all passed once for the same reason — and it had
simply moved from the facts path to the evidence path. `_holder_return_for` promised "a
complete set of the project's own descriptive pages" in its docstring while the code said
`if folder.primary_documents:`.

`sharia_evidence_screen.enough_was_read` is the floor, and it is `CORROBORATING_PAGES` —
**the same number a refusal already needs**. A screen that demands two pages to refuse and
accepts one to pass is not cautious, it is biased towards passing.

The gate sits **above** the branch that turns an unanswered question into a refusal.
That branch is right when a project described itself at length and still never answered;
applied to a single page it turns "we could not read it" into "there is something wrong
with it", and `U` was refused exactly that way before the ordering was fixed.

Measured before it was adopted: on the twenty coins of the blind run it moves **no** coin,
and on the ten new ones it moves exactly the one decided from a single page. A floor of
three would have moved two coins of the measured baseline, and three is not a number
anything here can justify.

**Three approved rules are contested among scholars**, and the register says so on each:
`GH-13` (liquidity pools and impermanent loss), `MY-07` (pay-to-play games with random
rewards), `PR-10` and `PR-12` (music, film, and hotels built on them). `GH-13` is the
widest: taken at its word it refuses essentially every decentralised exchange, including
ones other authorities accept.

**The 91.6% below still holds, and it no longer describes the whole product.** Both
statements are true, and the difference is which path is being measured.

That number comes from the *facts* path: a model was given a symbol and a name and wrote
down what each asset does, using the original thirteen activity words. Those facts never
say "alcohol" or "two sales in one", so the twenty new blocking activities cannot fire on
them and the measurement is untouched — `test_invariant_automated_screen.py` still passes
at 91.6% with seven wrongly eligible, and the SC Malaysia floor still holds.

The *evidence* path — reading a project's own web pages, which is what the product
actually runs — is where the new rules bite. It was blind-measured at 14/18 with five
blocking activities. With twenty-three it will refuse more coins, and some of them will
be coins Fasset publishes as compliant. **That is the intended consequence of a
deliberate decision, not a defect** — but it means the 14/18 figure describes the old
rule, and must be re-measured before it is quoted again.

The distinction the whole thing rests on:

> Earning from **lending money** blocks. Earning from **doing work** — validating,
> staking, running a service — does not.

That is why AAVE, COMP and GHO are refused while LDO, rETH and EIGEN are not.

## How it was measured

Fasset publishes 188 assets it calls compliant and 52 it calls not compliant. Those 240
labels are the only place in this product where **both sides** of a real authority's
line are visible, which is why the rule was derived from them and not from SC Malaysia
or the Shariah Review Bureau — both publish accepted assets only.

**The first measurement was thrown away.** Facts written by the same hand that knew the
labels scored 100%, which is not a result: the rule was rediscovering its own author's
answers. The honest test replaces the facts with ones a model wrote **blind** — given
only a symbol and a name, never told Fasset exists, never shown a verdict, never shown
which answers block. That is the production path: enrichment writes facts, the screen
reads them.

| Run | What changed | Accuracy | Wrongly eligible |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Facts written by the rule's author | — | 100% | 0 |
| Blind, round 1 | first honest test | 91.6% | 12 |
| Blind, round 2 | asked for holder return + governed business | 82.5% | 8 |
| **Blind, round 3** | **removed a duplicated rule** | **91.6%** | **7** |

Round 2 went **backwards**, and the cause is worth keeping: `interest_bearing_holding`
and `holder_return` had both been allowed to decide whether a return is riba. Two copies
of one rule, and they disagreed at once — Chainlink, Polygon, Hedera, NEAR, stETH and
rETH were all refused for paying a return that was payment for work. `HolderReturn` is
now the single owner of that question.

**Held: 91.6% on 227 decided assets, 7 wrongly eligible.** Pinned by
`tests/unit/test_invariant_automated_screen.py`, which fails if accuracy drops below 90%
or the wrongly-eligible count rises above 7.

### What it still gets wrong

Seven assets pass that Fasset refuses, and all seven are gaps in what the model *knew*,
not faults in the rule:

| Asset | The model said | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| USD0, AUSD, FRXUSD | "pays the holder nothing" | All three pass a yield to holders |
| CVX | "governs a spot exchange" | Convex's business is lending-style yield |
| WOO | "spot exchange" | Also runs perpetuals |
| FTN | "own network" | Fastex is a betting and gaming group |
| FLOKI | "consumer application" | Fasset treats it as a meme token |

A research step that reads each project's **own documentation** — which is what the
enrichment queue already does — closes most of this. Guessing from a name does not.

## SC Malaysia is the ruler, not an ingredient

SC Malaysia publishes no per-coin reasoning, so there is nothing in it to copy. Its
value is the opposite: it is the only **regulator** in the set, so its 15 approvals are a
floor the screen may never fall below.

`test_sc_malaysia_is_the_ruler_the_screen_is_measured_against` fails the build if the
automated screen refuses any asset the Malaysian Shariah Advisory Council approved. If
that test ever goes red, the rule is wrong — not the regulator.

The same shape can be reused for any authority: an approved list becomes a floor, and a
refused list becomes a ceiling. Fasset already supplies both.

## What must stay true

- It is **never** merged with an authority's result into one score.
- It publishes under its own name, and the notice travels with every result.
- An unanswered question is `insufficient_facts` — sent back for research, never shown
  as eligible.
- The wording lives in one partial and one Python constant. Two copies would drift, and
  a reader who saw the softer one would trust it.
