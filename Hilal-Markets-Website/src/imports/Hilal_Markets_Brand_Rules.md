Version 1.0 — 17 July 2026

This document is a working copy of the master reference for the Hilal Markets brand. It defines the principles that should guide the website, product interface, presentations, social content, illustrations, motion and future marketing materials.

## 1. Brand definition

Hilal Markets is a platform for Muslim traders to build strategies and monitor market setups in line with Islamic principles.

The platform supports research, strategy building, asset screening and market monitoring. It is not a broker, does not execute trades and does not provide black-box buy or sell recommendations.

Working descriptor:

> A platform for Muslim traders to build strategies and monitor setups in line with Islamic principles.

## 2. Core brand idea

Hilal Markets combines professional market tools with clarity, evidence and Islamic principles.

The brand should feel:

- professional, but not institutional or cold;
- technological, but not futuristic or artificial;
- Islamic in purpose, without relying on religious visual clichés;
- minimal, but not empty;
- distinctive, but not visually loud.

The central visual principle is:

> Rounded product. Chamfered brand accents. Clean space. Controlled colour.

Rounded forms structure the interface. The chamfer identifies the brand.

## 3. Brand personality

### Clear

Explain complex market and Shariah information in simple language. Prioritise direct meaning over technical terminology.

### Calm

Avoid urgency, hype, fear of missing out and aggressive crypto aesthetics. Hilal Markets should help users feel informed and in control.

### Evidence-led

Important statuses and alerts should be supported by a source, methodology, decision date, condition or evidence record.

### Principled

The product should consistently respect its published rules, product boundaries and selected Islamic principles.

### Transparent

Show how conclusions were reached. Communicate sources, dates, limitations, uncertainty, missing conditions and status changes openly.

## 4. Brand name and capitalisation

- Use **hilal markets** inside the official logo artwork.
- Use **Hilal Markets** in running text, legal text and product copy.
- Do not write **HilalMarkets** without a space.
- Do not write **HILAL MARKETS** as a stylistic treatment.
- Do not recreate or manually typeset the wordmark when an official logo asset is available.

## 5. Logo rules

Use the official logo without altering its proportions, geometry or spacing.

Allowed forms may include:

- horizontal logo;
- symbol only;
- monochrome dark version;
- monochrome light version;
- approved version on the apple-green background.

Do not:

- rotate, stretch or compress the logo;
- change the angle of its chamfered geometry;
- add shadows, outlines or gradients;
- place the logo on visually noisy backgrounds;
- use unapproved colours inside the mark;
- turn the complete logo into a repeated pattern.

Exact clear space and minimum sizes should be defined from the final logo geometry in Figma.

## 6. Chamfered geometry

The chamfer is derived directly from the Hilal Markets symbol. It is a brand signature, not the default shape of the interface.

Rules:

- Always use the exact angle taken from the logo.
- Use chamfers at a large or medium scale where the geometry remains recognisable.
- Use no more than one clearly visible chamfer treatment in a section or composition.
- Keep product cards, inputs and most buttons rounded.
- Do not apply chamfers to every card, button, icon or divider.
- Do not use decorative chamfers where they could be mistaken for an interactive control.
- Use the element less frequently in product screens than in marketing materials.

## 7. Brand frame

The frame built from parts of the logo is a large background or emphasis device.

Use it for:

- presentation covers;
- large statements;
- hero compositions;
- campaign graphics;
- a single focal area inside a marketing section.

Rules:

- Use it at a large scale with generous empty space.
- Keep it low contrast and secondary to the content.
- On apple green, use a slightly lighter green.
- On white or near-white, use a slightly darker tint from the same colour family.
- On black, use a subtly lighter neutral.
- Avoid placing small text directly over its strongest edges.
- Do not use the frame repeatedly within the same viewport.

The frame should usually feel like part of the background rather than a foreground illustration.

## 8. Trading-bars pattern

The trading-bars pattern is derived from the logo angle and the visual rhythm of market bars.

Use it selectively for:

- Join the waitlist sections;
- presentation covers;
- promotional banners;
- campaign graphics;
- rare high-emphasis sections.

Rules:

- The pattern must fade progressively into transparency.
- The first column is the most visible; later columns should become increasingly subtle.
- The pattern should remain secondary to the message and CTA.
- Do not combine it with dense market charts or data tables.
- Do not use it throughout the dashboard.
- Do not repeat it in multiple adjacent sections.

A useful starting rhythm for relative visibility is 100%, 65%, 35% and 10%, adjusted to the background so the final result stays subtle.

## 9. Colour system

### White and near-white

The product interface is predominantly white. Near-white and very light neutral surfaces may be used to separate cards, groups and sections without creating heavy contrast.

### Near-black

Near-black is the primary text colour. It may also be used for small high-contrast cards, buttons and labels.

### Apple green

Apple green is the primary brand accent.

Use it for:

- primary CTAs;
- selected labels;
- focused interface states;
- small typographic highlights;
- illustration accents;
- promotional backgrounds;
- a single focal element in a section.

Do not use bright apple green for small text on white. A darker accessible green should be defined for text and critical UI labels.

### Accent blue

Blue is a rare secondary detail, not a second primary brand colour.

Use it for:

- connectors;
- small progress details;
- minor illustration elements;
- rare supporting labels where appropriate.

Do not use blue as a major page background, headline colour or primary CTA colour.

### Suggested colour balance

As a general visual guide, not a strict mathematical rule:

- 70–80% white and near-white;
- 15–20% neutrals and near-black;
- 5–10% apple-green accents;
- less than 2% blue details.

Marketing slides and isolated campaign banners may use more apple green than the product interface.

## 10. Semantic colours and statuses

Brand colour and product status must remain conceptually separate. Apple green should not automatically mean that an asset is eligible or that a condition has passed.

Create dedicated semantic styles for:

- eligible;
- forming;
- ready for review;
- under review;
- excluded;
- warning;
- error;
- information;
- evidence updated.

Never communicate a Shariah or monitoring status through colour alone. Always pair colour with clear text and, where useful, an icon.

## 11. Typography

### Headings

- Typeface: **Geometria**.
- Weight: **Medium**.
- Use sentence case.
- Keep headings concise.
- Use generous line spacing and clear hierarchy.
- Avoid Bold and Extra Bold unless a future approved exception is documented.
- Do not use Title Case where every main word is capitalised.
- Do not use ALL CAPS for headings.

Correct:

> Monitor every setup

Incorrect:

> Monitor Every Setup

> MONITOR EVERY SETUP

### Body and interface text

- Typeface: **Onest**.
- Use Regular for body text.
- Use Medium for buttons, navigation, labels and controlled emphasis.
- Use heavier weights rarely.
- Use tabular numerals for prices, percentages and aligned financial data.

Uppercase is allowed when required by established technical notation, including BTC-USDT, ETH-USDT, API and official abbreviations. Status labels should normally remain in sentence case.

## 12. Layout and interface

- Use generous white space.
- Establish hierarchy through spacing, type scale and contrast before adding decoration.
- Use rounded cards, inputs and interface surfaces.
- Keep borders light and shadows minimal.
- Prefer one strong focal element per section.
- Use apple green to direct attention, not to decorate every component.
- Keep information-dense screens calmer than marketing materials.
- Do not allow brand patterns to interfere with charts, evidence or financial data.

## 13. Iconography and illustration

Use:

- simple vector icons;
- rounded icon containers where appropriate;
- official asset and communication-channel logos;
- product-interface compositions;
- evidence cards;
- subtle charts and progress visualisations;
- connectors that clarify a process;
- restrained use of the logo-derived angle.

Illustrations should explain a product flow or state. They should not exist only as decoration.

Do not use:

- AI brains or robots;
- glowing crypto spheres;
- generic futuristic networks;
- exaggerated candlestick imagery;
- decorative elements that resemble real market data;
- fake partner or trust logos.

## 14. Photography and cultural imagery

Do not use photographs of people as part of the core brand system.

Avoid visual clichés such as:

- mosques used as decoration;
- praying people;
- stock photographs of Muslim traders;
- gold coins and luxury imagery;
- excessive crescent symbols;
- generic Middle Eastern patterns unrelated to the logo.

The Islamic identity of Hilal Markets should be communicated through the name, product principles, evidence, language and trust system rather than literal religious decoration.

## 15. Motion

Motion should explain relationships and progress.

Use:

- calm sequential reveals;
- left-to-right product flows;
- subtle connector animation;
- progress between monitoring states;
- hover or click interactions that reveal evidence and details;
- restrained transitions between forming, completed and paused states.

Do not use:

- constant looping movement without meaning;
- aggressive flashing or glowing;
- fast trading-terminal effects;
- decorative AI-processing animations;
- motion that makes monitoring appear to be automated trade execution.

Always provide a reduced-motion experience.

## 16. Language: Islamic principles and Shariah

Use **Islamic principles** in accessible brand and marketing communication:

- hero copy;
- descriptors;
- general explanations;
- introductory sections;
- campaign messaging.

Use **Shariah** when technical or methodological precision is required:

- Shariah screening;
- Shariah methodology;
- Shariah-status change;
- Strategy Guard explanations;
- Evidence Passport details;
- product policies and review records.

Do not use the two terms randomly. The first introduces the purpose; the second explains the formal product mechanism.

## 17. Tone of voice

Hilal Markets should sound calm, precise, honest and helpful.

Prefer:

- screened;
- monitored;
- evidence;
- methodology;
- eligible under the selected policy;
- requires review;
- source updated;
- user-approved rules;
- condition passed;
- condition still missing.

Avoid:

- 100% halal;
- guaranteed halal;
- guaranteed profit;
- winning signal;
- risk-free;
- buy now or sell now;
- AI trades for you;
- Shariah approved, unless formally supported by the relevant authority;
- urgency, fear and exaggerated financial promises.

When something is uncertain, say so clearly. When a source provides limited reasoning, disclose that limitation.

## 18. Accessibility

- Ensure text and controls meet accessible contrast requirements.
- Do not use bright green as small text on white.
- Do not rely on colour alone for status or meaning.
- Provide visible focus states.
- Keep interactive targets large enough to use comfortably.
- Use readable text sizes and line lengths.
- Provide reduced-motion behaviour.
- Ensure charts and notifications include text explanations.

## 19. Practical do and do not

### Do

- Use one clear apple-green focal point.
- Use the brand frame as a large, subtle background device.
- Keep product surfaces rounded.
- Use chamfers as rare recognisable accents.
- Show evidence, dates and status explanations.
- Let product UI and typography carry the visual identity.
- Keep dense information screens visually quiet.

### Do not

- Chamfer every button and card.
- Turn blue into a second primary colour.
- use bright green for inaccessible small text.
- place decorative bars behind real charts.
- use several brand patterns in one section.
- use ALL CAPS or Title Case for interface headings.
- use generic Islamic or crypto visual clichés.
- imply that Hilal Markets executes trades.
- imply an absolute religious guarantee.
